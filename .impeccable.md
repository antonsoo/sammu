# Sammu design context

The owner supplied the audience and purpose: fluent English speakers learning about ancient musical traditions, including ancient Mesopotamia. They want substantive, surprising learning and distinctive open-source projects; they explicitly reject generic showcase filler.

Chosen use case: work through a documented retuning procedure, hear the pair that changes, and investigate what a seven-name cycle does and does not establish. The experience should make the source, its scholarly reconstruction, and our audible numerical realization distinguishable at every step.

Design direction: an editorial instrument bench. Warm plaster, deep blue ink, rust actions, and restrained teal and rose for the source's clear/unclear distinction. Large numbered strings are the controls, with a precise pitch trace beside each. The physical string schematic is our own diagram, not a reconstruction of an excavated instrument. Put diagnosis and listening first; unfold the exact fractions, route planner and evidence afterward. No fake clay textures, invented ancient performances, decorative waveforms, dashboard statistics or card gallery. Self-host Atkinson Hyperlegible Next for controls and Noto Serif for headings and Akkadian romanization. Preserve keyboard focus across all retuning actions, respect reduced motion, and make the 375 px layout a purposeful vertical workbench.
